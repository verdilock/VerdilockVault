# The Update You Kept Skipping Could Cost You Everything

---

We get it. Software update notifications are easy to dismiss. They pop up 
at the worst possible moment, right when you're in the middle of something 
important, and clicking "remind me later" becomes second nature. Most of 
the time nothing happens. Until one day it does.

That's not a scare tactic. That's exactly what CISA, the United States 
Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, just reminded millions of 
people about. Outdated versions of Microsoft Excel are currently leaving 
individuals and businesses across the country exposed to cyberattacks that 
are entirely preventable. Not because of sophisticated hacking operations. 
Not because of state sponsored attacks. Simply because of a skipped update.

## What CISA Actually Said

In April 2026 CISA issued a warning directed at users still running 
unpatched versions of Microsoft Excel, some dating as far back as 2007 
and 2008. That's nearly two decades of accumulated security vulnerabilities 
sitting quietly on millions of devices right now.

The concern isn't just theoretical. Outdated software contains known 
vulnerabilities, weaknesses that have already been discovered, documented 
and in many cases actively exploited by attackers. When a vulnerability 
becomes public knowledge it essentially becomes a roadmap for anyone looking 
to cause harm. Patching your software closes that roadmap. Skipping the 
update leaves it wide open.

## Why This Matters More Than You Think

Here's something the cybersecurity industry doesn't always communicate well. 
Attackers are not always sophisticated. They are opportunistic.

The image most people have of a cyberattack involves a hooded figure in a 
dark room running complex code against a high value corporate target. The 
reality is far more ordinary. A malicious spreadsheet file lands in your 
inbox disguised as an invoice, a job application or a shared report. You 
open it. Your unpatched system processes it. Your system is now compromised 
over something entirely preventable.

This attack method is not new. It is not rare. It is one of the most 
consistently used techniques in cybercrime precisely because it works. And 
it works most effectively against systems that haven't been updated.

Small businesses are particularly vulnerable. Not because they are targeted 
more specifically but because they are often least equipped to recover from 
the consequences. A data breach for a small business owner doesn't just mean 
lost files. It can mean lost clients, lost revenue, damaged reputation and 
in serious cases permanent closure.

## How to Check Your Excel Version Right Now

Here are three simple steps:

**Step 1.** Open Microsoft Excel on your device.

**Step 2.** Click **File** then **Account** then **About Excel**. On older 
versions click **Help** then **About Microsoft Excel**.

**Step 3.** Check the version number. If you are running anything earlier 
than Excel 2010 you need to update immediately. Visit 
[microsoft.com](https://www.microsoft.com) to download the latest version 
or enable automatic updates through your Microsoft 365 settings.

That's it. Three steps. 60 seconds. Done.
